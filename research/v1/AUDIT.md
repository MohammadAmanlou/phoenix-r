## Proposed research object
For a specified population, initial verified false claim, frozen information environment, AI policy pi, horizon T, audit budget B, and target audience, define success as discovery of verified counter-evidence, transmission to target, and correction by a predefined fraction of audience within T. Estimate separately for true claims (false correction guardrail). Distinguish ordinary correction from intervention-assisted correction; do not infer general internet behavior from a lab.

Proposed contribution is intervention-defined auditability of claim-level repair under joint hidden failure and endogenous evidence, plus a learning policy that must preserve a robust lower bound. It is not a new union probability identity. Core empirical question: do policies with matched static accuracy and matched low-order audit performance differ materially in joint recovery, and can measured joint audits reveal or prevent this? The closest existing robust influence, verification incentives and selective-label methods must be implemented with equal budget. If they solve the same estimand, narrow to the causal measurement study or abandon the algorithm claim.

## Exact counterexample and bounds
Let X_j be whether a concurrently executable, pre-specified repair lane succeeds in a frozen claim scenario. The portfolio succeeds if any lane succeeds, ONLY in the noninterfering OR benchmark. World E uniform on 000,011,101,110; O uniform on 001,010,100,111. Both have all single and pairwise distributions identical. Union probability E=.75, O=1. Independence estimate .875 in both. Marginal-only sharp interval [.5,1]; pairwise sharp interval [.75,1] via exact Hailperin LP and analytic inclusion-exclusion. An alternative portfolio with known .875 success yields opposite optimal decisions; any selector using only identical low-order data has worst-case expected regret at least .0625 (randomizing equally attains it in this two-world toy).

General k>=2: uniform even and odd parity laws on binary vectors have identical proper-subset marginals (uniform). Even law assigns 2^{-(k-1)} to all-zero; odd assigns zero. Thus portfolio gap is 2^{1-k}; midpoint comparator yields worst-case regret >=2^{-(k+1)}. For mixtures with odd parity probability theta, success is 1-(1-theta)/2^{k-1}. In fresh iid scenario audits with at most k-1 route outcomes per scenario, no information about theta; complete joint audits reveal Bernoulli parity. If two mixtures theta+ and theta- require opposite decisions and pass probabilities >=1-beta versus <=alpha, standard KL chain rule and data processing give E_plus[N_full] >= kl(1-beta,alpha)/kl(theta+,theta-), assuming no other theta-dependent observations, finite stopping expectation and fixed audit mechanism. This is a restricted derivation from known methods, not a theorem-priority claim.

Joint audits must measure executable lanes in the same scenario, not unobservable joint counterfactuals of one person. In real networks, simultaneous lanes can interfere, compete for capacity or change behavior; use direct randomized portfolio evaluation rather than an OR assumption, and separate discovery and delivery interventions. The direct full-portfolio audit is the essential strong baseline. Adaptive deployment requires independently held-out or valid sequential audit evidence.

## Reproducible computational check
`python repair_audit.py` uses numpy/scipy, seed 20261126. 8 constructed worlds x 4 sample sizes x 4000 = 128000 independent synthetic trials (two methods use the same generated counts). No LLM inference or human data. Exact LP and all mixture moments checked by assertions. Direct Clopper–Pearson intervals use observed portfolio outcomes; structural intervals transform parity intervals under exact mixture structure. Comparator .875 known by construction (not estimated from data), equal full-vector audit sample sizes, no real cost claim. Structural violation places .2 on 000 and .8 on 111; this intentionally breaks the moment assumptions. It is a stress test, not evidence that such a distribution occurs in real networks.

At n=100, true .775: correct rejection 75.28% direct vs 100% structural; true .975: correct adoption 96.25% direct vs 100% structural. At true .875, direct and structural adoption rates 1.32% and 1.55%. Under structural violation (true .8), structural false adoption 100% and coverage 0%; direct false adoption 0/4000, coverage 96.90%, correct rejection 54.85%. At n=400 direct correct rejection 98.40% in violation. These are Monte Carlo frequencies, not guarantees of zero error. Full results CSV includes coverage, widths and decision regret. No claim of superiority over a direct audit in real-world settings.

## Empirical plan and kill criteria
1. Reproduce relevant fixed-evidence source dependence and cascade baselines. Construct paired fictional claims with authoritative ground truth and hidden independently obtainable evidence; matched initial outputs and provenance. 2. Randomize AI policy by independent network room, with randomized independent evidence acquisition and delivery support; record discovery, verified counter-evidence, reach, uptake, and actual final correct belief separately; correct-claim controls. Full factorial contrasts measure policy effects and interaction, not natural mediation without additional assumptions. 3. Use a jointly executable audit portfolio or separate randomized direct portfolio evaluation. Pilot power at room/claim level, not individual click count. Ethics, informed consent, debrief, synthetic closed environment; no open-web misinformation posting. 4. Compare direct portfolio audit, robust influence optimization, exact oracle, marginal and pairwise LP, independent cascade, existing credibility prompts, active acquisition, and policy with randomized exploration. 5. Kill or revise algorithm claim if low-order information suffices empirically, direct audit is equally efficient, methods fail calibration under distribution shift, or higher-order dependencies cannot be measured. A rigorous null is valuable. 6. Only claim conference readiness after reproducible results, full priority comparison, human ethics and actual experiments, stress tests, and feedback from Nicholas.
